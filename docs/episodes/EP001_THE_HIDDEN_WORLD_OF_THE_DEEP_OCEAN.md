# FACT BITES — Episode 001 Production Brief

## Working title
**The Hidden World of the Deep Ocean**

## Format
- Main documentary: 8–12 minutes, 16:9.
- Language: English.
- Narration: original, warm, low-register male documentary delivery; no imitation of a real presenter.
- Visual target: native 4K where rights-cleared footage exists; otherwise native 1080p with honest labeling.
- Follow-on assets: 3–5 vertical Shorts cut from the approved master.

## Editorial premise
Take viewers from the sunlit surface into the deep ocean, revealing how light, pressure, food, and life change with depth. The story should feel like an expedition, not a list of disconnected facts.

## Proposed chapter arc
1. **The blue frontier** — the scale of the ocean and the descent from the surface.
2. **Where the sunlight ends** — the twilight and midnight zones; explain depth and light carefully.
3. **Life under pressure** — adaptations without sensationalizing or implying all deep-sea species are alike.
4. **An ecosystem built on scarcity** — marine snow, scavenging, and food webs.
5. **Oases in the darkness** — hydrothermal vents and chemosynthesis, with scientifically precise explanations.
6. **A world still being discovered** — exploration limits, what remains unknown, and why deep-sea ecosystems matter.

## Visual acquisition requirements
Acquire a varied set of rights-cleared shots: open-ocean surface, underwater descent, twilight-zone animals, deep-sea landscape, bioluminescent organisms, marine snow, hydrothermal vents, and research submersible/ROV exploration. Prefer original agency footage from NOAA Ocean Exploration, NOAA Fisheries, NASA, or other sources whose specific asset reuse terms are confirmed.

Do not begin final rendering with a single repeated source clip. Each chapter needs multiple distinct shots, with a clip ledger and visible credits. Verify the actual resolution of every downloaded asset.

## Research requirements
Use primary research and authoritative sources (NOAA, peer-reviewed papers, Smithsonian Ocean, MBARI, and relevant research institutions). Maintain claim-level source notes. Verify depth-zone boundaries and avoid presenting conventional ranges as hard biological borders. Explain chemosynthesis accurately and distinguish it from photosynthesis.

## Narration tone
Measured, vivid, intelligent, and human. The narrator should guide the viewer through discoveries, leave room for images and natural sound, and avoid exaggerated claims such as “scientists know nothing about the deep sea.”

## Production status
**Pre-production brief only.** No footage rights have yet been cleared for this episode, and no final script or finished film should be represented as complete until research, footage, narration, and QA gates pass.
