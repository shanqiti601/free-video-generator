# FACT BITES — Cinematic Documentary Standard

## Editorial direction
FACT BITES is a nature and science documentary channel. The visual ambition is premium natural-history filmmaking: patient observation, immersive environments, precise natural sound, carefully paced narration, and a strong scientific story. This is an original production identity, not a copy of any broadcaster's footage, scripts, music, logo, presenter, or distinctive sequence.

## Format strategy
- Main films: 16:9 landscape, target 8–12 minutes for the first episodes.
- Discovery cuts: 3–5 vertical Shorts (9:16) derived from each approved long-form film.
- One researched master script is the source of truth for the film, narration, chapter beats, fact cards, and Shorts.
- Never stretch one short clip into a long documentary. Each scene needs a distinct, relevant, rights-cleared shot.

## Picture standard
- Prefer genuine 4K UHD (3840×2160) source footage where available and legally reusable.
- Preserve source resolution. Never label upscaled 1080p as native 4K.
- If the source is 1080p, export a clean 1080p master rather than inventing detail.
- Use 16:9 framing, restrained cinematic grade, natural color, stable shots, and deliberate cuts.
- Reject low-resolution, watermarked, heavily compressed, irrelevant, or rights-unclear footage.
- Avoid synthetic AI wildlife shots presented as real animal behavior. If AI-generated imagery is ever used, clearly treat it as illustrative.

## Narration and sound
- Use an original English documentary narrator voice: warm, low-register, calm, articulate, and expressive; never imitate a named presenter.
- Narration must be mixed clearly above ambience and music.
- Use original or explicitly licensed music only. Do not use Planet Earth/BBC music or ripped soundtrack material.
- Build a natural sound bed from rights-cleared field recordings or restrained original ambience. Avoid a constant synthetic drone as a substitute for a real soundscape.
- Use pauses and dynamic range; do not read every fact at the same speed.

## Story structure
1. Cold open (0:00–0:20): a remarkable image and a compelling question.
2. Promise (0:20–0:45): what the viewer will discover.
3. Chapters: each adds a new visual or scientific idea; no repeated filler.
4. Evidence: explain what is known, how it was measured, and what remains uncertain.
5. Emotional resolution: return to the opening idea with a meaningful conclusion.
6. End card: FACT BITES identity only, unless a platform-specific CTA is deliberately approved.

## Research and rights gate
Every factual claim must be traceable to primary research or a high-authority scientific institution. Keep a source ledger with title, publisher, URL, access date, and the exact claim supported. Distinguish observation, estimate, hypothesis, and uncertainty. Never invent a citation.

Every video/audio asset must have a recorded source URL, creator/agency, license or reuse basis, and required credit. Publicly viewable does not automatically mean public domain. If rights cannot be established, do not use the asset.

## Quality gates
- Editorial: strong opening, coherent arc, no padding, correct pronunciation.
- Science: claim-by-claim fact check and source ledger.
- Visual: inspect representative frames from every scene; confirm framing, focus, resolution, relevance, and no accidental black frames.
- Audio: listen to the opening, one middle passage, and ending; check voice intelligibility, clipping, loudness balance, and sync.
- Technical: verify codec, aspect ratio, frame rate, duration, audio stream, full decode, and file integrity.
- Delivery: MP4 plus research sources, footage credits, narration script, and QA report.

## Non-negotiable constraints
- No paid APIs, subscriptions, trials, or paid credits without explicit approval.
- No claiming 4K unless the final image dimensions and source provenance support native 4K.
- No claiming a film is complete until the MP4 is downloaded and visually/audio inspected.
- Keep the existing short-form workflow intact; the long-form workflow is a separate production path.
